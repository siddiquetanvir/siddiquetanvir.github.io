Research Vision & Inquiries

Investigating the intersections of low-resource natural language processing, morphological complexity, and community-curated open knowledge graphs.

My intellectual focus lies at the boundary where rigorous systems programming meets linguistic diversity. While modern machine learning models demonstrate impressive fluency in dominant global languages, the digital preservation and computational modeling of low-resource and endangered languages remain critical bottlenecks. As an undergraduate student in Computer Science & Engineering at Khulna University of Engineering & Technology (KUET), my objective is to ground myself in core algorithmic theory while pursuing questions in computational linguistics, open access corpora, and tokenization fairness.

"The true measure of linguistic artificial intelligence is not how fluently it mirrors high-resource data, but how gracefully it adapts to languages that have historically been excluded from digital ubiquity."

1. Low-Resource NLP & Morphological Bottlenecks

Standard large language models (LLMs) operate on tokenization schemes (such as Byte-Pair Encoding and WordPiece) that are heavily biased toward morphologically simple or high-resource languages like English. When applied to agglutinative or complex inflective tongues—such as Basque (Euskara) or Fulfulde—these tokenizers fragment words into arbitrary byte fragments, resulting in inflated sequence lengths, higher computational latency, and severe semantic degradation.

My inquiries in this area focus on:

  • Morphology-Aware Representations: Investigating character-level and byte-level subword architectures that avoid fragmentation penalties in low-resource settings.
  • Cross-Lingual Knowledge Transfer: Exploring how structural linguistic priors and typological data (e.g., from WALS or Glottolog) can guide parameter-efficient fine-tuning without requiring vast parallel corpora.
  • Open Digital Archiving: Building reproducible scraping, normalization, and OCR cleanup pipelines to digitize folklore, regional dictionaries, and historical vernacular literature into benchmark datasets.

2. Large-Scale Taxonomy & Open Knowledge Infrastructure

Before algorithms can perform reliable reasoning, they require verified, structured representations of reality. My background operating automation scripts across Wikimedia platforms (managing over 46,000 automated edits and categorizing 15,000+ media files) demonstrated to me the indispensable role of open semantic metadata.

I am interested in exploring how community-curated ontological graphs (such as Wikidata and Wikipedia entity networks) can serve as verifiable grounding mechanisms for machine learning systems:

  • Automated Taxonomy Validation: Using Pywikibot and SPARQL logic to detect contradictions, orphan categories, and cyclic dependencies in multilingual knowledge trees.
  • Neuro-Symbolic Integration: Studying hybrid approaches where neural text extractors are constrained and verified by graph taxonomies, minimizing hallucination in factual retrieval tasks.

3. Academic Foundations & Trajectory at KUET

To build lasting solutions rather than ephemeral wrappers, I believe strong fundamentals in mathematics and systems engineering are non-negotiable. At KUET, my immediate priorities include:

  • Systems & Algorithm Rigor: Mastering memory management, data structures, and asymptotic optimization in C/C++ to implement high-throughput data pipelines from first principles.
  • Mathematical Frameworks: Deepening my understanding of linear algebra, discrete mathematics, and probability theory to comprehend loss landscapes and optimization guarantees in deep learning.
  • Community & Club Engagements: Actively participating in algorithmic contests with the KUET Programming Society (KUETPS) and exploring hardware-software interfaces with HACK.
